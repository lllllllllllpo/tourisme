Opening — Introduction
The first 60 seconds set the tone for the entire conversation
💡 The goal is to put the prospect at ease immediately, show they're in good hands, and create trust and personal attention. Warm, brief, and professional.
Customize Your Trip — Prospect brief
What Ombline says

"Hello [First name], this is Ombline calling from Paris to Versailles Private Tours.

It's wonderful to have you on the line. My role here is to take the time to understand what you'd like to experience — so we can build a journey that truly feels like yours, not just a standard package.

I have a few questions for us to get acquainted — is now a good time for you?"

Wait for confirmation before continuing. If it's not a good time, immediately offer an alternative: "When would be a good time for me to call you back?" and note it below.
1
The Trip
Dive in with empathy — let the prospect talk
🎯 This is the most important section. Listen without interrupting. The questions below are conversation prompts — not an interrogation. Let them lead.
What Ombline says
"So, tell me everything — what kind of trip do you have in mind?"
Q1 What matters most to you on this trip?
Don't rush. Reflect back what you heard: "So if I understand correctly, what really matters to you is..."
What Ombline says
"And what inspired you to plan this trip now — is there a special occasion?"
Q2 Is there a special occasion?
If it's a surprise, carefully note what the recipient should NOT know. If it's a honeymoon, think immediately about personal touches: champagne, suite, flowers.
What Ombline says
"Wonderful. Have you visited Paris or Versailles before?"
Q3 Have they visited Paris / Versailles before?
A returning traveller expects more exclusive experiences — private access, behind-the-scenes, lesser-known spots. Adapt your pitch accordingly.
2
The Group
Understand who you're working for
What Ombline says
"So we can personalise everything — how many people will be travelling?"
Q4 Group composition CYT: Number of adults / Age range / Children
Number of adults
Age range of adults
Children
Group composition drives everything: accommodation, pace, transport, accessibility. Always ask about young children and reduced mobility without waiting for them to bring it up.
Q5 Contact details CYT: Name / Email / Country / Phone
Country of residence is a key data point: visa requirements, travel habits, typical budget. American and Australian clients often have higher budgets and precise service expectations.

Dates & Logistics
Qualify how mature the project is
What Ombline says

Q6 Planned dates CYT: Trip length / Month / Year / Exact dates
Trip length
Dates
Precise dates with purchased flights = highly qualified prospect. Vague dates = still exploring — adjust the depth of your proposal accordingly.
Q7 Arrival logistics CYT: Transportation
Preferred transport (Paris → Versailles)
Naturally suggest a private driver for high-end clients or special occasion trips — don't wait for them to ask.
4
Budget & Travel Style
After trust is built — never ask this first
⚠️ Never bring up budget first. This section comes naturally after understanding the project, the group, and the dates. By now, trust is established — the budget question feels like a formality.
What Ombline says

"So we can put together a proposal that's truly tailored to what you're looking for — do you have a rough budget in mind per person, excluding flights?

We work across a wide range, from a few thousand dollars to fully exclusive experiences — there's no right or wrong answer, it just helps us calibrate our suggestions."

Q8 Budget per person (excl. flights) CYT: Budget per person / Budget flexibility
If the prospect hesitates, offer two options at different levels. The accommodation spontaneously mentioned is often the most reliable indicator of real budget.
Q9 Travel style & accommodation CYT: Accommodation / Tour type / How do you like to travel
Level of accommodation
Types of tours
Preferred pace
Accommodation details
Honeymoon or special occasion clients almost always choose private. Communicating rooms are often forgotten for families — anticipate this.

Private tours

Q12 What can we do to make this trip special? CYT: What can we do to make this trip special?
This is an open invitation — listen without interrupting. Often the most important detail for the proposal comes here.
6
Comfort & Accessibility
Prevent unpleasant surprises
What Ombline says
"One last practical thing — are there any specific constraints we should keep in mind? Mobility, dietary needs, young children..."
Q13 Mobility & accessibility
Important for Versailles (very large estate). Anticipate internal transport options (golf cart, PMR access) if needed.
Q14 Dietary restrictions or health needs
Never skip this. An unanticipated allergy or accessibility issue can ruin the experience and damage the agency's reputation.
7
End of Call — Trust & Competition
Once the relationship is established — light touch, never intrusive
🤝 These questions come at the end of the call, once trust is built. They help understand the competitive context and gauge real intent. Frame them lightly — never as an interrogation.
What Ombline says
"Have you had a chance to speak with other agencies, or are we the first ones you're talking to?"
Q16 Other agencies consulted
Stay curious and warm — not defensive. "And what was it about that proposal that didn't quite fit?"
Closing script

"Thank you so much — this has been really helpful.

I'm going to take the time to put together something truly personalised for you.

I'll send you [a full proposal / a sample itinerary] within [timeframe]. And if you have any questions in the meantime, don't hesitate to reach out directly.

Speak soon — have a wonderful day!"
